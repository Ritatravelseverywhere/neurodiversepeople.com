Why this autism test includes a masking section

Most free autism tests online ask whether you display autistic traits — but they don't account for the fact that many autistic adults, particularly women, have learned to hide or suppress those traits so effectively that they score below the threshold on standard tests.

This test includes a dedicated Social Strategies section that measures how much effort you put into adapting, compensating, and concealing in social situations. This is the only free autism test in the UK that screens for masking as part of its core assessment.

What is masking?

Masking is when autistic people hide or suppress their autistic traits to fit in — copying others' body language, scripting conversations, forcing eye contact. It's effective at hiding autism from clinicians, but exhausting to maintain. High maskers often score lower on standard tests despite significant autistic traits. Learn more about masking →

Signs of autism in UK adults

Autism in adults often looks very different from the childhood stereotype. Common signs in adults include:

  • Finding social interaction genuinely exhausting, even when you're good at it on the surface
  • Missing implied meaning, sarcasm, or hints in conversation
  • Strong sensory sensitivities — to sound, light, texture, smell
  • Intense, focused interests in specific topics
  • A strong need for routine and significant distress when plans change
  • Feeling like you're performing or acting in social situations
  • A lifelong feeling of being different without knowing why

What does an autism screening test actually measure?

A screening questionnaire is not a diagnosis — it's a structured way of comparing your lived experience against the patterns clinicians look for. Formal autism assessment in the UK is built around two broad areas defined in the diagnostic manuals (DSM-5 and ICD-11):

  • Social communication and interaction — differences in reading social cues, interpreting implied meaning or tone, maintaining back-and-forth conversation, and building or sustaining relationships in the way that's typically expected.
  • Restricted and repetitive patterns — a strong preference for routine and predictability, intense focused interests, repetitive movements or habits that help with regulation, and sensory sensitivities (to sound, light, texture, taste or smell) that can be either overwhelming or under-responsive.

In the NHS, the most common brief screening tool is the AQ-10 (a 10-item short form of the Autism Spectrum Quotient), recommended by NICE as a first step to decide whether a full assessment is warranted. It's deliberately short, so it's a signpost rather than an answer — and, importantly, brief tools like the AQ-10 can underscore high-masking adults, which is exactly why this test adds a dedicated masking section.

Autism is lifelong. Diagnosis looks for traits that have been present since early childhood, even if they went unnoticed or were explained away as shyness, sensitivity, or being "a bit different." A good screening result encourages you to reflect across your whole life — not just how you feel right now.

How to interpret your autism screening result

If your result indicates significant autistic traits, it means your answers align with the patterns commonly seen in people who go on to be diagnosed — not that you're definitely autistic. A formal diagnosis can only be made by a qualified clinician, usually a multidisciplinary team, after a detailed assessment of your developmental history and current functioning.

If your result sits below the threshold, remember that standard screening can miss people who mask heavily. If you strongly recognise yourself in the experiences described here despite a lower score, that recognition is still worth taking to your GP — self-identification is valid, and a lower questionnaire score doesn't invalidate a lifetime of feeling different.

After your free autism test — UK next steps

If your results indicate autism traits above the threshold, the next step is speaking to your GP. Take your printed results and describe how these traits affect your daily life — at work, in relationships, and at home.

NHS waiting times for adult autism assessment can be long — sometimes several years in some areas. Private autism assessments are available and typically cost £800–2,500. The National Autistic Society has a directory of assessment services at autism.org.uk.

Could you have AuDHD — autism and ADHD together?

Around 50–70% of autistic people also have ADHD. If you think both might apply to you, this test screens for both simultaneously. When both are indicated, your results will show an AuDHD profile. Read more about what AuDHD is and how to get assessed.

Autism in women — a note

Women are diagnosed with autism on average 10 years later than men. This is largely because autistic women tend to mask more effectively, have interests that are socially acceptable, and present with anxiety and depression rather than obvious autistic behaviours.

This test is specifically designed to catch high-masking presentations. Read more about signs of autism in adults.

Frequently asked questions

Can an online test diagnose autism?

No. No online test — including this one — can diagnose autism. A diagnosis requires a comprehensive clinical assessment, usually by a specialist team. What a screening test can do is help you recognise patterns, decide whether a formal assessment is worth pursuing, and give you something concrete to discuss with your GP.

Is this autism test free, and is my data kept private?

It's completely free with no sign-up and no email required. We don't store your answers or results anywhere. You can print or save your result as a PDF at the end to keep for yourself or bring to a GP appointment, but nothing is saved on our servers.

Why does the test take up to 35 minutes?

The core screening is short, but the test is adaptive and offers an optional extended "Life Experiences" section that explores social connection, language, sensory experience and routine across your lifespan. Taking the longer path gives a richer picture, especially for high-masking adults — but you can stop at the core result if you prefer.

What if I mask heavily and score low?

This is common, particularly among autistic women and adults diagnosed late. This test includes a dedicated masking section precisely because standard tools can underscore people who have learned to camouflage their traits. If you recognise yourself strongly despite a lower score, it's still worth raising with your GP.

Can I be autistic and have ADHD?

Yes — the combination is often called AuDHD, and around 50–70% of autistic people also have ADHD. This test screens for both at once, so if ADHD traits appear alongside autism traits your results will reflect an AuDHD profile. You can also take our free ADHD test.
